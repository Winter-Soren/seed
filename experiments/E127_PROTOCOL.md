# E127 · Can a creature use evidence that its food has changed?

## Question and subjects

Compare the three completed E103 plastic controllers and the corresponding E113 fixed-weight recurrent controllers. Each creature gets identical opportunities to experience both colors. Measure whether it changes its choice after their meanings reverse and whether it uses that experience when searching a fresh grid. There is no evolution, demonstration or fitted readout in this experiment. E126 genomes and worlds are excluded. Randomizing where two alternatives appear is also used in published reversal-learning tasks. Seed uses its own deterministic outcomes and guided experience. [Hampton, Bossaerts and O’Doherty (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1010-06.2006)

## Guided experience

Keep the original 103 observations, five actions, 48 hidden units, two neural microsteps, reward magnitude, decay and energy bounds. One creature occupies an empty hub on the original-sized grid. For each trial the apparatus places A and B in two distinct cardinally adjacent cells. It directs the outward move and the return to the empty hub. On the return tick the unconsumed item is still visible. The next pair is presented after the return. The body receives normal food or poison and decay from the actual moves. The brain receives the imposed action's motor copy and its actual outcome on the next neural tick. Chamber item placements are controlled presentations, distinct from ordinary random item growth. The creature sees no rule or reversal signal.

Choose the script independently of controller and rule. Every two successive contacts include one of each color. Each eight-contact block uses all eight contacted color/direction combinations. Randomize pair order, direction assignments and the other item's location independently of the rewarded color. For each family replay both initial rules against the same script, birth and random draws. Balance the hidden boundary at eight or sixteen contacts across the panel (two of each in development, 64 of each in finals). Follow with sixteen contacts. The boundary follows the return tick, after the last pre-boundary outcome has reached the brain. The controlled schedule keeps the body alive without energy clipping. Verify this for every assigned family.

Clone the full pre-boundary state. In one clone the rule reverses, in another the old rule continues. For the plastic controller add a third clone with reversal and Hebbian weight changes disabled starting on the return tick that first processes a changed contact outcome. Keep predictor updates and recurrent activity. The direct controller uses the two intact conditions. Its weights are fixed, while its recurrent activity can change. All snapshots preserve the weights, activity, predictor and structural mask.

## Two readouts

At zero, two, eight and sixteen post-boundary contacts, take the state after the return tick. On independent, discarded clones, present all twelve ordered placements of the two colors at distinct cardinal neighbors. Use fresh neutral body fields (energy one, incoming energy change zero, no other agent), preserve the full brain, and freeze both weight updates for this one readout tick. Read the existing five-action softmax. No move or reward feeds back into the source. Record all five probabilities, both item actions, probability of approaching either item and probability of any other action. Zero-contact stationary and reversal readouts must agree byte for byte.

After sixteen contacts also place the unprobed brain into a new ordinary grid. Standardize body, position, episode clock and counters. Preserve the neural activity of both architectures, including that of the fixed-weight control. Pair the target layout and action randomness across conditions, always using the reversed rule as the target. Resume ordinary free behavior for 50 turns, retaining the Hebbian intervention in the third arm. Record first food, first poison, no contact, alive, contact latency and net return. Score every assigned life. A death before contact and no contact both score zero for first-food success. A trained state can be affected by the fresh body, elapsed time, context, search and sensory differences. A weak grid readout cannot isolate navigation as the sole cause.

## Prospective decisions

The unit of uncertainty is one independent family. Average the twelve placements and both initial-rule replays inside each family before calculating means and standard errors (sample SD divided by the square root of the family count). Use paired family differences for each contrast. Report every controller and seed separately. The primary time is sixteen post-boundary contacts. The intermediate measurements are descriptive.

Define **S** as the probability of moving toward the reversed target food minus the probability of moving toward the other color. Score the same target in the reversal and stationary clones. A lineage passes local reversal only if (1) before the boundary it prefers the old food, with −S greater than two SE, (2) after reversed feedback S is at least 0.10 and greater than two SE, and (3) reversed minus stationary S is at least 0.10 and exceeds two paired SE. A replicated local result requires at least two of three lineages of the same architecture. A modest feedback effect can occur without reversal of an established preference. Report it separately.

For transfer, **G** is the all-assigned probability that the first contact within fifty turns is current food. A lineage passes transfer if reversed minus stationary G is at least 0.10 and greater than two paired SE, and its reversal probability of food minus poison is greater than two SE above zero. Replicated local-to-grid transfer requires the same two or more lineages to pass both stages. For Hebbian dependence, state a local contribution only if the same two plastic local-pass lineages show an intact-minus-frozen S advantage of at least 0.05 and more than two paired SE. Report the analogous transfer contrast descriptively. A failed threshold means this assay did not establish the corresponding effect. It cannot establish architectural impossibility, a general adaptation score or the outcome of E126.
