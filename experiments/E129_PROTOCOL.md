# E129 · Do continued synaptic updates during the empty interval weaken the learned preference?

E128 found that preference survived 33 active turns without either item but failed its registered allowable-loss criterion in all three original plastic lineages. E129 tests one cause. It changes when an existing rule is permitted to update synaptic weights. It introduces no new rule or evolved genome.

## One intervention

Use the E128 six original controllers, acquisition, fresh-body probes, standardized solo grid, counterbalanced initial rules and balanced eight/sixteen pre-boundary lengths on **new** families. Use four development families with seed 2026120800 and 128 independent families per lineage with seeds 2026120801, 2026120802 and 2026120803. Separate seven random streams as in E128 with assignment tag `0xE129`. Verify the original controller digests. Keep E127 and E128 results and worlds separate.

For each plastic creature and acquisition history (reversal or stationary), clone the complete acquired state. In one clone all ordinary updates remain active. In the other, disable only Hebbian W updates for the 33 turns on the empty grid. Start the intervention on the **first empty neural turn**, including the processing of its incoming energy-change signal, and end it immediately after turn 33. Predictor weights continue updating throughout. Preserve sampled moves, action feedback, energy cost, perception, recurrence and random inputs in both clones. Actions and internal states may diverge as a consequence of the treatment; this is a total effect of the intervention. Use the same ordinary 1+32 continuation in both. Verify W's bit identity against the acquired value at the input and output of every frozen turn. Probe copies use E127's read-only adjacent-item test. Re-enable normal lifetime learning in all plastic solo-grid readouts. The unchanged direct controls need only one run for each history because their W is fixed throughout. Validate that the immediate readouts agree between the two plastic treatments. The only behavioral endpoints are zero and 33 turns.

## Scientific decisions

S is the local probability of moving toward the currently safe color minus that of moving toward the other color. G is the all-assigned first-food rate by fifty solo turns. Y scores first food +1, first poison −1 and no contact or death before contact zero. As in E128, average both initial rules in each family and average twelve placements for local S. Every change first pairs within an independent family. Report means and sample standard errors for individual lineages.

1. **Effect of the intervention.** In every plastic lineage, frozen-interval minus normal-interval S in the reversal history at 33 turns must be at least 0.025 and strictly greater than two paired SE.
2. **Local rescue.** In those same three lineages the frozen-interval arm must pass every E128 local criterion. At both endpoints, S after reversed feedback and the difference in S from stationary feedback must each be at least 0.10 and greater than two respective SE. The lower bound for S at 33 minus zero, computed as the paired mean minus two SE, must be strictly above −0.05.
3. **Transfer rescue.** In the same three local-rescue lineages, reversal-history Y must exceed two SE at zero and 33. The delayed G advantage over stationary history must be at least 0.10 and greater than two paired SE, and the paired lower bound for G at 33 minus zero must exceed −0.05. The delayed reversal-history G of the frozen-interval arm must additionally exceed normal-interval G by more than two paired SE.

Report the intact and direct controllers and all failed components. Also report absolute rates and the actual size of the causal effects. Overlapping bounds leave the registered claim unestablished. A successful intervention would show what an engineered update schedule does in this assay. It would leave unresolved whether a creature can learn when to use that schedule in its ordinary environment.
