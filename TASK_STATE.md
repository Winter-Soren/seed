# Current state

Updated 22 November 2026. Follow PLAN.md and AGENTS.md.

## What we know

All three original A genomes improved their food choices within a stationary lifetime. Among creatures reaching both meals, first-to-twentieth meal accuracy rose by 27, 21 and 25 percentage points, with standard errors near two points. They struggled after a food-rule change. A fixed-weight recurrent baseline won the original average score in all three seeds.

E116 found smaller evolutionary steps beat larger ones, but neither justified replacing the original genomes. E117 tested a different kind of inheritance. Newborns given donors' acquired connection changes reached shelter 38.4, 44.6 and 37.3 turns sooner than fresh-start siblings, with paired standard errors of 5.3, 5.4 and 5.4 turns. Survival increased in all three lineages. Most arrivals happened before cold, leaving the specific content of the archive open.

E118 let all three original genomes mutate for ten generations with the persistence trait active. Their descendants saved −6.6, −8.5 and +5.0 turns versus their parents, with paired standard errors of 6.0, 6.4 and 6.9 turns. All met the survival safeguard and none met the shelter improvement threshold. All ten updates per lineage and all held-out results passed independent reconstruction.

E119 continued the three saved E118 genomes from generation ten through generation twenty. Every generation received real acquired weight changes from its parent cohort. Against its immediate parent, the final descendant saved −2.1 ± 3.1, −40.8 ± 6.3 and +6.3 ± 6.0 turns. No lineage cleared the improvement threshold. All three descendants reached shelter more slowly with their accumulated archive than when the same genome started fresh. Absolute survival was low.

E120 gave newborns a movie of a practiced forager before they acted alone. Teachers chose food on about 81–83% of late encounters. Newborns chose food first in 67, 66 and 64 of 128 solo lives. Watching beat every control in zero of three lineages. The independent reconstruction confirmed the decision.

E121 showed a reader could infer the food color from the teacher's public movements in all three lineages. Accuracy was 84.4 ± 2.3%, 84.4 ± 2.5% and 89.8 ± 1.9%. Even the moving scenery retained much of the cue. The original newborns' difficulty is using the information available in these movies.

E122 selected the observers for first-food success after watching. All ten rounds changed every genome parameter in each lineage. Descendants' gains over their own starting observers were −4.7 ± 4.7, +7.0 ± 4.3 and +6.3 ± 5.8 percentage points. Zero lineages passed.

E123 added one calibrated food-color memory to the original newborns. Keeping that memory improved the first independent choice versus erasing it by 32.8 ± 4.3, 24.2 ± 4.1 and 36.7 ± 4.3 percentage points. All three lineages beat five controls and met the survival safeguards. The readout was engineered and its reliability calibrated from each lineage's own experience.

E124 found that the same lesson survived three behavioral handoffs in all three lineages. Third-cohort first-food rates were 86.7 ± 3.0%, 83.6 ± 3.3% and 86.7 ± 3.0%. Only the third lineage improved beyond both the first cohort and replaying the original teacher. Cumulative improvement failed to replicate.

E125 changed which color was food once during a pupil's life. In the late window the socially prepared pupils chose food first in 12.5 ± 2.9%, 18.0 ± 3.4% and 20.3 ± 3.6% of assigned worlds. When the rule stayed fixed, the same pupils scored 78.1 ± 3.7%, 69.5 ± 4.1% and 71.1 ± 4.0%. Keeping the old lesson also hurt survival. Every lineage failed the recovery test. The two types of unprepared controller also struggled after the change.

E127 gave creatures sixteen matched encounters with a changed food rule. All three original plastic lineages reversed their preference and carried it to a fresh grid. Their first-food advantage over old-rule experience was 58.6 ± 3.2, 58.6 ± 3.4 and 59.0 ± 3.3 percentage points. Hebbian weight changes contributed in all three. The fixed-weight recurrent controls passed in zero of three. Every assigned life counted.

E128 found that all three still preferred the changed food after 33 empty turns. First-food success fell by 9.0 ± 3.7, 7.8 ± 3.5 and 4.7 ± 3.3 percentage points. None met the registered five-point retention margin for either local choice or independent foraging. The fixed-weight controls showed little immediate learning.

E129 found that pausing changes to connection strengths for those empty turns preserved local food preference in all three lineages. With ordinary learning restored, their solo first-food gains over continued updating were 9.4 ± 3.9, 12.1 ± 3.1 and 11.3 ± 3.1 percentage points. A0 and A1 also retained immediate foraging performance within five points. A2's lower bound was a loss of 5.03 points. The full three-lineage transfer claim failed.

E130 tested each founder against its generation-200 descendant when all four creatures foraged freely. All three descendants learned the old rule and recovered some ground after the switch. Their late first-item scores relative to their founders were −0.096 ± 0.048, −0.064 ± 0.038 and −0.111 ± 0.041. None met the evolutionary recovery criterion. Two lineages crossed the council's threshold for long gaps without contact.

E131 compared feedback-timed exploration with ordinary foraging and the same quota at random times. It passed the laptop cost criterion at 1.32× the ordinary warm median and failed behavior in all three lineages. A2 found food 3.42 ± 1.10 turns sooner than ordinary foraging. No lineage reliably beat equal quota on time to first food, and late adaptive Q was −0.051 ± 0.031, −0.041 ± 0.031 and −0.070 ± 0.034. Retire this setting.

E132 made both colors easier to reach while keeping every action voluntary. The three lineages sampled both colors 71.1 ± 3.8, 68.0 ± 3.9 and 72.7 ± 4.0 percentage points more often. Solo first-item scores improved relative to random-distance offers. Stationary worlds showed similar gains, and survival during reversed exposure fell by 13 to 16 points. None met the reversal criterion.

E133 compared native updates with a locally observed contact gate, an equal-dose random schedule and reopening updates during the empty gap. All three gated lineages still favored the old food immediately after eight reversed contacts (S −0.471 ± 0.008, −0.454 ± 0.009, −0.450 ± 0.008). Native brains still favored it too. Reopening weakened that preference. After the gap, gated solo scores fell by 0.484 ± 0.080, 0.125 ± 0.085 and 0.516 ± 0.079 versus native. The gate failed in all three lineages. Retire this setting.

E134 evolved the native 155-number genomes for ten attempts per lineage and tested each frozen endpoint against its parent on 128 fresh families. All three showed positive local reversal preference after the gap, but none passed the 0.05 direct improvement criterion. Direct solo changes were −0.094 ± 0.061, −0.172 ± 0.066 and −0.176 ± 0.063. The independent audit passed and the improvement gate failed 0/3.

E135 changed only whether extra items appeared in the first observation with the same adjacent A/B, physical scene and acquired state. On 128 fresh families per lineage, the masked stationary gains replicated at +0.183 ± 0.010, +0.131 ± 0.010 and +0.132 ± 0.014. Full-view attenuation reached +0.390 ± 0.022, +0.122 ± 0.018 and +0.312 ± 0.020. The registered gate passed 3/3. The full-view gain was erased in A0 and A2. A1’s remaining gain is unresolved. The joint audit reconstructed 384 final and four development families. [Audited results](results/diagnostics/E135-independent-audit.json)

## What is running

No experiment is running. E126 training stopped at generation 50 of 200 in all six lineages. An independent check reconstructed every genome update. All 155 inherited values in each plastic lineage and all 8,424 in each recurrent lineage differ from their founder. The full comparison projects to 16 hours 53 minutes against the 12-hour ceiling. The comparison on fresh worlds remains sealed.
